## Executive Summary

Two to four sentences. What is this, why does it matter, and what outcome are we driving toward? Written so someone who wasn't in the kickoff understands the initiative without further context. No jargon, no solutioning — the what and why, not the how.

## Problem Statement

**What isn't working**
The current problem or friction in concrete terms — behaviour, numbers, specific breakdowns. Not vague dissatisfaction.

**Why it matters**
The business and user cost of leaving this alone, and the value of solving it. Tie back to the stated business goal.

## Users & JTBD

**Primary user segment(s)** *(hypothesis until Evidence validates)*
Named segment(s), and if known, roughly how many people or what proportion of users.

**Job(s) to be done** *(hypothesis until Evidence validates)*
The underlying need, not a solution restated as one — phrase as "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]". If the kickoff gave a feature request instead ("add a filter"), push one level deeper: the filter is a solution, the need underneath it is the JTBD.

**Known pain points** *(hypothesis until Evidence validates)*
Each pain point, with its source: research, support tickets, sales feedback, or one stakeholder's opinion. An unattributed pain point is still recorded — write "source: not stated" rather than dropping it. An unattributed pain point is itself a hypothesis for Evidence to test, not a reason to leave it out.

## Current State

What happens today — the existing flow, system or experience the problem lives inside. Factual, not evaluative. This is the baseline any future state will be measured against.

## Proposed State

*Not defined at Intake.* Articulating the target future state is descoped to **Analyse-Explore (Stage 3)**, which fills this section once recommendations have been generated from validated evidence and a direction has been chosen. Defining it here would anchor the whole project to a solution before Evidence has had any chance to shape it.
